# Source notes

What's been learned about each drafting book, so the next design from it doesn't pay to learn it
again. Facts about the books only. Add a line whenever a book surprises you. Each design's `BRIEF.md`
has the detail for its own garment.

Sources are cited by corpus folder id ([corpus/MANIFEST.yaml](corpus/MANIFEST.yaml)). **PDF page = book
page + offset.** Confirm the offset once by looking at a printed page number: some scans shift.

## All scans
- The OCR drops or confuses fractions ("\ l/2" for 1½, a blank for ¼). Read numbers from the image.
- A contents page can be wrong. Search the text for the garment's name.

## Sheifer 1908 (`nssheiferssystem00shei`)
- Skirt coat: text PDF p. 80, plate PDF p. 81. The text starts the skirt about 2 in below the waist (at the cutter's choice), places the front seam about 1 in lower than the back, leaves ¾ in between front and side gore 1½ in below the waist, and shapes a hip dart at B. The plate shows a curved, dropped skirt seam.

## Merz 1911 (`merzspracticalcu00merz`)
- Diagram XXIV, Inverness: text book p. 58/PDF p. 60, plate book p. 59/PDF p. 61. Its body references Diagrams XVIII, XXII and XXIII. OCR drops the cape's 3/4-inch darts and reads the collar's 1/4-inch spring unreliably; verify the page image.
- Diagram XIV, riding frock coat: text book p. 38/PDF p. 40, plate book p. 39/PDF p. 41. Diagram XVII, three-piece long coat: text book p. 44/PDF p. 46, plate book p. 45/PDF p. 47. Check the printed page number; the diagram number alone does not identify a PDF page.
- Diagram XIV text gives 36 in total length, ¾ in lapel extension, and 20½ in half hip including ½ in ease. The skirt is swept from point 6; the source does not give a numeric hem sweep. The back opening is opposite J at the waist.
- Diagram XVII text (PDF p. 46) gives a quarter waist cutout at 12–A, another quarter waist at B–E, a ⅜ in hip increment on a 20 in hip, a ¼ in back enlargement, and ¾ in from 10–M and 11–V. The plate (PDF p. 47) carries the shaped vertical divisions from the shoulder and armhole down to the long hem; some thin diagonals are construction and transfer lines rather than cut edges. It gives no overall length, pocket placement, or fastener count.

## Schorr 1917 (`expertdesignerpr00scho`)
- Diagram 65 has four panel shapes across A–G on the half skirt: front on the fold plus three cut in pairs. The 18 in outline to 13 in half waist removes ½ in at front point 1, 2½ in at side point 5, and 2 in near back point 2. Points 1 and 2 are 3 in from the waist ends, and their hem ends 5 in from the hem corners.
- Diagram 7 text (PDF p. 38) says C–G is half the 42 in hip plus **½ in**, so 21½ in; B–C is 6 in and point 6 is 1 in from point 5. The plate (PDF p. 39) shows the radial half panel and the hem wedge closed into a central waist dart. Length and finished bottom width are left to choice; no numeric hem width is supplied.
- Diagram 7, Basque or Tunic Skirt: text PDF p. 38, plate PDF p. 39. The worked 26 in waist/42 in hip example has a radial outline and a wedge closed into a dart. Diagram 65, Four Gore (Panel) Skirt: text PDF p. 94, plate PDF p. 95. Plate pages do not show a printed page number in these examples.

## d'Alessio 1906 (`artofdesigningcu00ales`)
- Cyclists' breeches, Fig. 30: figure PDF p. 73, text printed p. 68/PDF p. 74. The plate has a full leg gathered into a separate band. The English text gives 27 in total length, 23 in to the knee line, 21½ in around the knee, 30½ in around the thigh, a 16 × 3 in band and a 2 in raised **back** waist (F on the back piece). Its 27 in = 62½ cm conversion is wrong; use inches. Titan's back waist already rises about 2 in at the proportion-40 model.

## Weiler 1915 (`weilerladiestail00weil`)
- Riding Habit Coat: text printed p. 82/PDF p. 86, plate printed p. 83/PDF p. 87. It starts from a 40 in hip; the back is 1¼ in at waist and 1¾ in at hip, with a 1¾ in optional back slit. The preceding tight-fitting coat is text/plate PDF pp. 84–85. OCR confuses these fractions, so read the page image.

## Simons 1922 (`designingboysjuv00simo`)
- **"1/6 of breast" can mean a sixth of the half breast.** In Diagram 10's neck it's 2 in on a 24 in
  breast, not 4. Check with the plate's scale.
- Diagram 10 (plate p. 41, text p. 42) reuses Diagram 9's sleeve and cuff without redrawing them.
  Plates may face or precede their text, so a text search can miss them.
- The contents are wrong for the raincoat: Diagram 36 is on pp. 105–107, not p. 118.
- The "kilt" (pp. 50–52) is a single flared foundation, with no pleats or wrap.
- Diagram 13's heavy diagonal from point 10 is the lapel folded over the body. Lapels are often drawn
  folded: don't read that line as a cutaway.
- Heavy crossing lines from the armhole go to the *other* panel's side, and the two flares can differ.
- Diagram 43, "golf pants", is fitted through the knee, not plus-fours.
- Diagram 44, scout pants, is full length to the shank with a V tuck across the back knee and a back hem pleat; the text is on pp. 122 and 124, with the plate on p. 125.
- Diagram 38 is the full-length cape overcoat, pp. 110-112, not p. 126 (which is long trousers). Its plate shows a whole back and separate fronts, not a short shoulder cape over a coat.
- Diagram 7's Norfolk has a front and back with a side seam, not a three-piece body.
- Diagram 23, pp. 74–75 (PDF pp. 80–81), puts the single- and double-breasted reefer on one back and forepart. The text says the double-breasted edge is 2½ in from centre front and its button line 4 in from that edge; the plate also marks a lower pocket without explaining its construction.

## Simons 1918, sack coats (`designingsackcoa00simo`)
- Diagrams I–III (pp. 5–7, PDF 9–11) are the base the book asks you to memorise: a back with a CB seam and a
  forepart running under the arm, no side body or dart. The forepart's side seam crosses the back's below the
  waist and overlaps it 2¼ in at the seat line.
- His widths include the seams' make-up, which he doesn't separate out. 33, the front edge, is 1½ in past 32:
  read as a stand with CF in its middle, as his 1922 boys' book states for its 1¼ in stand.
- No buttons, breast pocket or vent are drawn on Diagram III. The size 36 draft is 30 in long; the proportions
  (p. 47, PDF 51, printed sideways) give 31.
- The seat line (E) is only 5½ in below the waist, much higher than a modern waist-to-seat: scale lengths below
  the waist from the wearer's own measurement, not his.
- Diagram VI (double-breasted, text p. 10, plate p. 11) and VIII (two-button English, text p. 14, plate p. 15)
  re-letter the construction lines (B–G, with D the chest line and E the waist) and add front Vees. Diagram VII
  (three-button, "high suppressed waist") is between them and isn't queued.
- Diagram X, the tuxedo (text p. 18, plate p. 19), is the English coat with a 1 in stand, one button on the waist
  line and the front cut away below it; the lapel is drawn folded over the forepart.
- Diagram XII, the full dress coat (text p. 22, plate p. 23), letters its lines B–H with E the "fashionable" and F
  the natural waist; the size of the Vee at 19 is lost in the scan ("x inch").
- Diagram XI, the frock coat (text p. 20, plate p. 21), is "the basis for all body fitting garments": its skirt
  front slopes from the forepart's point to ⅓ of the breast from the back at the hem (a cutaway).

## Simons 1916 (`designingovercoa00simo`)
- The shoulder cape overcoat is on pp. 30-31, not p. 21. It is a full-length cape with a military collar and a whole back; the 7 in button stand is a cut-on front extension.
- The contents name p. 19 "French", but it's the form-fitting overcoat's diagram. Pp. 42–44 are a mackinaw.
- Diagram XXI's plate is faint. Its text doesn't place the buttons.

## Mitchell 1921 (`newstandardworko00mitc`)
- Riding skirts: divided skirt text/plate on PDF pp. 120-121 (book pp. 116-117); apron riding skirt Part I on PDF pp. 122-123 (book pp. 118-119), Part II on PDF pp. 124-125 (book pp. 120-121). The divided draft has a narrow, separate full-length front panel alongside two pairs of divided gores, not a two-panel culotte. The apron draft has separate Part I and Part II pieces joined by a buttoned leg strap.
- Text and diagrams alternate: in the Prince Albert jacket (pp. 48–51), text is on PDF 52 and 54, and
  diagrams on PDF 53 and 55.
- His skirt fans about 60° on p. 51, on a corseted figure. A modern figure's seat less waist gives less.
- Some manipulations of a plain skirt, such as the high-waisted one, are variants, not designs.

## Vincent 1890 (`vincentmakingshirts18xx`)
- "D.B." in the smoking jacket's text is one row of frogs on a wide overlap, on the plate.
- Collars and cuffs for several shirts are shared on p. 23.
- Painter's jacket is book p. 35, PDF p. 39: 36 in breast, 34 in long from neck level, 17½ in to waist; the text supplies five buttons and the cook's jacket sleeve. Its three pocket sizes and collar height are marked on the plate.
- Eight work aprons are together on book p. 39, PDF p. 43; the text continues on p. 40. The diagrams give fixed inch dimensions, not a graded draft. Diagram 8 is the painter's apron, distinct from the painter's jacket on p. 35.

## Pivnick 1949 (`pivnickdesignclothes1949`)
- Figures carry no numbers: read proportions, with the scale from a known length such as neck point
  to waist, and say so in the brief.
