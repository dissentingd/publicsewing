# Garment tradition marker audit

Scope: 109 built PublicSewing designs with a spec and brief, reviewed 2026-11-13. A design
may have several markers: the national drafting lineage of its cited source, a named garment
tradition, and a specific source uniform or design. These are associations with our reference
or interpretation, not claims of exclusive cultural ownership or authentic reconstruction.
The wardrobe tree's form and function categories are separate.

## First pass: 100 designs marked

The cited drafting publications supply a national **reference tradition** for 96 designs:
73 American, 17 British, and 7 Soviet-era CMEA associations. One design cites both an
American and a British reference. These labels appear as garment tradition
markers, not as a second kind of source-origin badge. ЕМКО vol. 3 was
[issued in Moscow in 1988](https://rusneb.ru/catalog/000200_000018_rc_426814/), and all seven
derived designs carry the USSR flag. The label describes that volume's drafting lineage;
the CMEA method and the garments circulated across several Eastern Bloc countries.

Three designs have a specific source tradition in their briefs:

| Design | Additional tradition | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| `field-trousers` | British Army CS95 field uniform | Cited Open+Pattern digitization of the CS95 Field Combat Trouser. |
| `anorak` | US tactical smock | Its cited Open+Pattern source uses that name; official issue is not established. |
| `jumpsuit` | Thayaht's TuTa, Florence 1920 | The brief and Uffizi Galleries identify the specific design. |

Six named styles have an initial garment-tradition association. Their briefs establish the
garment name and construction; the national association is a first-pass classification to
check against garment-history sources before adding more detailed period or community claims.

| Designs | Named tradition | Qualification |

| `kilt` | Scottish kilt | Mostly our own design; the brief expressly says it is not a traditional reconstruction. |
| `balmacaan` | Scottish Balmacaan coat | Based on Simons' American rendition of the named coat. |

This yields 100 marked designs because five of the six named styles also inherit a book's
national reference tradition. The labels coexist; one does not replace another.

## Nine designs without a marker

`t-shirt` (a generic Open+Pattern source), and eight of our own designs on FreeSewing
blocks: `cardigan`, `fitted-dress`, `henley`, `joggers`, `princess-dress`, `shell-top`,
`turtleneck`, `wrap-dress`. A block's location is not a garment tradition. Research their
particular design influences before adding a cultural, national, period, social, or religious
label.

## Linked catalog entries

The 28 linked Open+Pattern rows were reviewed separately after an omission in the first
audit. Their own names and descriptions explicitly identify a national military or workwear
reference for 25 entries, so those now have flag icons too. This includes the German Army
Bundeswehr reversible snow jacket, Finnish, Swedish, Swiss, British, and US examples, and
the TuTa jumpsuit's Italian source. The three generic T-shirt entries remain unassigned.
These markers describe Open+Pattern's source objects; they do not mean PublicSewing drafted
those linked patterns. The 59 FreeSewing listings still need a separate tradition review.

## Next history pass

Check other named forms (`mackinaw`, `peacoat`, `ulster`, `prince-albert-jacket`,
`smoking-jacket`, `middy-blouse`) against garment-history references and their briefs.
For every addition, record the tradition's own name, community or region, period where
known, and what PublicSewing changed. A garment can belong to more than one tradition;
religious, cultural, social, and fashion-wave labels need icons that represent them well.
