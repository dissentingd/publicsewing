# Women's Inverness coat

**Source:** Merz, *Practical Cutting System for Ladies' Jackets and Cloaks* (1911), Diagram XXIV, text p. 58 (PDF p. 60), plate p. 59 (PDF p. 61), `merzspracticalcu00merz`. The referenced tight back, transferred shoulder and loose front are in Diagrams XVIII, XXII and XXIII. US 1911, public domain.

**Status:** draft. Not sewn.

## What Merz gives

Merz calls this a loose-fronted, tight-back, three-quarter Inverness with a turn collar closed in front. The plate shows a long shaped body under a separate shoulder cape. The armhole is lowered 1 inch. The cape extends 2 inches beyond the shoulder, with two small 3/4-inch darts separated by 2 inches. Its length is to taste. The cape front sits 1/2 inch from point 25 and 3/4 inch from point 9. The collar rises 3 1/4 inches, with 1/4 inch spring at its outer edge; its front end is 2 1/2 inches. The text does not specify pockets, buttons, or a sleeve.

## How this draft is made

The body reuses the Breanna-based women's box coat: separate shaped back, loose front with a neck dart, long flared side seams, facing, and button closure. The `closed` front option runs the top edge from the neck to the front edge without a lapel. The separate one-piece cape uses the same block's neck and shoulder, extended by `capeShoulderExtension` (5.6% of chest, 2 inches at 36 inches), and a circular hem. Two marked shoulder darts are each `capeDartWidth` 2.1% of chest (3/4 inch), with `capeDartGap` 5.6% (2 inches) between their mouths, and `capeDartLength` 7.3% (2 5/8 inches) to their tips. Its default length is 57% of the coat's neck-to-hem distance, estimated from the plate. The coat length is 66% of waist-to-floor below the waist, also estimated from the plate. The collar width is 24% of neck, about 3 1/4 inches at the reference size; spring is 2% of its seam length, about 1/4 inch. The 5 buttons and 16% chest ease are our choices.

## Differences and limits

The body retains the box coat's side seam geometry. It does not transfer the shoulder as Merz instructs, lower the armhole by 1 inch, or reproduce his tighter waist and hip back divisions. The cape remains a circular approximation of Merz's sweep; its two darts are marked on the cut piece but do not reshape the outer edge after sewing. The shared collar has constant width and a simple outer spring, rather than Merz's separately dimensioned front and back heights. The two-piece sleeves and pockets of the parent are omitted: neither is on the Inverness plate. These differences need a muslin before fit claims.

## Pieces and sewing

Cut two coat fronts, two shaped backs, two facings, one cape on the fold and one collar; add the cape's facing and interfacing as preferred. Sew the body shoulder and side seams, close the front neck dart, and finish its front edges with the facings. Sew the two marked cape shoulder darts, finish the curved hem and front edges, then secure it at the neck under the collar. Sew the collar, hem the body, and place buttons on centre front. Check the cape's clearance over the arm and adjust its length and shoulder extension in a muslin.
