# Footman's pantry jacket

**Source:** W. D. F. Vincent, *The Cutter's Practical Guide to Jacket Cutting* (London, 1897), "Footman's Pantry Jacket," printed p. 54 (PDF p. 53), Plate 24 Diagram 2 (PDF p. 52), `cutters-prac-guide-jacket-cutting`. Public domain; this supplied PDF has no searchable text layer.

**Block:** FreeSewing Bent through the built `eton-jacket` and its three-piece `boys-suit-jacket` parent. Vincent calls this a three-seamer with an Eton-style front, a narrow whole back, and two forepart welt pockets. The existing body and pocket operations cover those elements.

**Threadloop:** Searches for a footman's pantry jacket and free Eton-style jackets found no equivalent drafted from measurements.

**Status:** Draft, not sewn.

## What the source shows

Plate 24 draws a whole back, a side body and a forepart. The forepart has a deep folded lapel, four buttons and two narrow welt mouths; its hem is straight and short. The diagram's waist station is marked 17 and its hem 22, making the lower body about 5 inches. The text describes the jacket as workwear for footmen, stewards and waiters, often in striped cotton with cotton lining, with the underarm fish omitted for ease and covered buttons on brass rings.

## Changes and what is not modelled

The inherited jacket has a shaped side body and a cut-on-fold back, but its back is not as narrow as Vincent's plate. The source's removable ring buttons and its omitted underarm fish are construction instructions, not separate pattern pieces here. The two-piece sleeve, collar, facing, lining and an optional inside pocket come from the parent; Plate 24 does not draft those pieces. Pocket and button positions are approximated from the plate.

## Pieces

Front (1), whole back (2), side body (3), top and under sleeves (4, 5), collar (6), facing (7),

## Construction

1. Mark the welt mouths P on the fronts (1). Make the lower welts (10) and pocket bags (11) while the fronts are flat; press and check that both mouths align.
2. Join each front (1) to its side body (3) at B, then the side bodies to the whole back (2) at C. Press the seams open.
3. Sew the shoulders A of the fronts (1) and back (2), and press them open.
4. Sew the collar (6) to the neckline E of the fronts (1) and back (2), with the back-neck facing (13) behind it, then the facings (7) to the front edges F. Turn and press.
5. Assemble each sleeve: join the top sleeve (4) to the under sleeve (5) at the front seam G and the back seam H, and press open.
6. Set each sleeve (4, 5) into its armhole D across the front (1), side body (3) and back (2), checking its pitch against the armhole marks; press toward the sleeve.
7. Make the lining from the front and back linings (14, 15), with the inside welt (16) and bag (17) if fitted. Attach it, leaving an opening to turn the jacket; close that opening after turning and pressing.
8. Turn and stitch the hems J of the body (1, 2, 3) and sleeves (4, 5). Work the four buttonholes BH on the fronts (1) and sew on the buttons. For a washable cotton version, use the source's lightweight lining and sew the buttons so they can be removed.
