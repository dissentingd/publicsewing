# Boys' reefer

**Source:** Simons, *Designing Boys' and Juveniles' Clothing* (1922), Diagram 23, book pp. 74–75, PDF pp. 80–81, corpus `designingboysjuv00simo`. The source's size 12 has a 29 in breast and 30 in coat length. US 1922, public domain.

**Block:** FreeSewing's Brian, through the peacoat and mackinaw. The design uses the wearer's measurements and is available at child and adult sizes.

**Status:** draft. Not sewn.

## What the source shows

Diagram 23 gives single-breasted and double-breasted outlines on the same back and forepart. The forepart has a long, straight double-breasted edge, three button positions, a turned lapel, a regular collar, a slanted chest welt, and a second slanted pocket mark below the waist. The back has a centre seam and the side seams spread toward the hem. Simons drafts the 29 in breast at size 32, allowing 3 in fullness. The double-breasted edge is 2½ in from centre front; its buttons are 4 in from that edge. The length is 30 in.

## How it maps onto the block

| Feature | Source | Here |
|---|---|---|
| Ease | 3 in on a 29 in breast | `chestEase` 11%; `bicepsEase` 25% is our allowance for a jacket underneath |
| Length | 30 in at size 12 | `lengthBelowWaist` 175% of waist to seat, about 14 in below a 16 in waist length |
| Front | 2½ in from centre front, with buttons 4 in from edge | `buttonExtension` 8.6% of chest; `buttonRow` 60% of the extension, leaving 1½ in between the button and centre front |
| Buttons | Three positions on the plate | Three pairs, from near the chest line to below the waist |
| Collar | Regular collar and turned lapel | Narrower `ulster` collar and lapel on the peacoat's block |
| Pocket | Slanted chest welt | The block's welt moved above the waist and shortened |
| Hem | Level, with spread at the side seams | Level; the Brian coat block has less flare |

## Changes and what isn't modelled

- The source's single-breasted outline is not a variant: the inherited double-breasted front has two button rows, and a single-breasted version needs a different front draft.
- The plate's second, lower pocket mark is omitted. The inherited block supplies one pair of welts, and its welt leans mostly vertically rather than the source's nearly horizontal chest welt. Adding another pocket or changing the welt angle needs a separate placement and bag piece.
- The exact side-seam flare, centre-back shaping, and source collar construction are simplified to the Brian coat block. The source does not detail a lining or vent, so both are off.

## Pieces and construction

Back, front, facing, collar, sleeves, and welt and pocket-bag pieces. Sew the welts and bags to the fronts; join back and shoulders and sides; add facings and collar; make and set sleeves; finish hem and buttonholes. The front is cut in mirrored pairs and closes right over left.
