# Riding frock coat

**Source:** Merz, *Practical Cutting System for Ladies' Jackets and Cloaks* (1911), Diagram XIV, printed pp. 38–39, PDF pp. 40–41 (`merzspracticalcu00merz`). Public domain. **Status:** draft; not sewn.

## Source and draft

The text gives a 36 in coat length, a ¾ in lapel extension, a skirt drawn separately by sweeping its waist, hip and hem from point 6, and a 20 in half hip plus ½ in ease. The skirt joins the fitted upper coat at the waist. The plate shows a long back seam curving into the armhole, a broad swept hem, a back opening near the waist, and a turned lapel. It does not give a numeric hem sweep or pocket dimensions.

The draft uses the fitted jacket's Breanna block and the existing one-piece waist skirt construction. `lengthBelowWaist` defaults to 48% of waist-to-floor, about 20 in on the reference size, yielding approximately the source's 36 in total length. `buttonExtension`, `lapWaist`, and `lapHip` default to 2.1% of chest, about ¾ in at a 36 in chest. `seatEase` is 2.5%, the source's ½ in on a 20 in half hip. `skirtSpread` is 7% of chest at each laid-out skirt division, read as a broad sweep from the plate; `fuller` raises it to 11%, our alternative.

Merz supplies no separate sleeve dimensions here; the inherited two-piece jacket sleeve and collar are used. The inherited front and underarm divisions approximate the plate's upper coat. The back is cut in pairs because the shared waist-skirt construction includes a center-back seam, although Merz puts it on the fold. The plate's back opening is omitted. Pockets and decorative pleats are omitted. These changes need a muslin before fit or riding comfort can be claimed.

## Construction

Sew the upper front, side front, underarm, side back and back seams. Join the skirt to the upper coat at the waist, matching division marks. Sew shoulders, collar and facings; assemble and set the two-piece sleeves. Add lining and close the skirt hem and front. Sew buttons along the front edge.
