# Divided riding skirt

**Source:** Mitchell, *Ladies' Tailor-Made Garments* (1921), "Divided Riding Skirt," book pp. 116–117, PDF pp. 120–121, corpus `newstandardworko00mitc`. US public domain. The text describes a five-gore skirt that resembles an ordinary skirt while walking.

**Block:** FreeSewing's Sarah through the existing five-gore skirt layout (`gored-skirt`). Its narrow front panel and two gore pairs provide the source's three distinct pattern shapes. The front and back divided gores reuse the culotte curve operation, now able to target any gore index.

**Status:** draft. Not sewn.

## What Mitchell states and draws

The text traces a separate front panel (points 10–14–12–6), a front side part (10–23–25–18–17–21), and a back part (20–17–18–28–27–16). Each side part is cut in a pair. The plate shows a short front crotch extension from the center line and a much longer back curve for the seat. The waist is shaped at the panel and side-gore seams. The front panel reaches the hem and covers the front division.

For the source's 40-inch hip, point 23 is ½ inch less than one quarter of the half hip: 4½ inches from the front line. Point 27 is ½ inch more than half of the half hip: 10½ inches behind the back line. The front fork is 13 inches below point 1; the rear is 1⅓ inches lower. The OCR loses some fractions, so those values were checked against the plate and text image.

## Mapping and changes

| Feature | Source | Draft |
|---|---|---|
| Five-gores | Narrow front panel and two pairs of gores | Sarah-based `gored-skirt` layout with three pattern shapes |
| Front extension | 4½ in on 40 in hip | `frontExtension` 11.25% of hip |
| Back extension | 10½ in on 40 in hip | `backExtension` 26.25% of hip |
| Front fork | 13 in below waist | `crotchDepthShare` 118% of the 11 in modern model's crotch depth |
| Flare | Broad skirt shape in plate | Parent's `goreFlare` 70% of half seat at full length |
| Length | Long, near floor in plate | Parent's `length` 95% of waist to floor |

The front panel is **attached at the waist and hangs free below**, overlapping the front split. Mitchell does not specify that attachment. This is a construction choice made for this draft. The divided gores are separate at the front below the waist; the panel is a modesty overlap, not a third leg seam. The modern Sarah block and proportional options replace Mitchell's point-by-point 1921 foundation. The rear fork uses the same depth as the front in this draft; the source lowers it by 1⅓ inches. The back's larger extension supplies seat room but does not reproduce that extra drop. The parent skirt's CB zip and waistband are ours; Mitchell does not give an opening or waistband in this section.

## Pieces and construction

Cut one front panel on the fold, two front side gores, two back gores and one waistband on the fold. Join front and back side seams, then the inseams and crotch. Attach the front panel at the waist, hanging free over the front division, and finish its free edge and hem. Close the CB seam and zip, attach the waistband, and hem the divided skirt. Fit the panel's hang and riding clearance in muslin before cutting final cloth.
