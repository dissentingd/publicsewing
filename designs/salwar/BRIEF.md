# Salwar

**Source:** inspired by Central Board of Secondary Education with the National Institute of Fashion
Technology, *Basic Pattern Development: Students Handbook + Practical Manual, Class XII* (CBSE, Delhi,
2014), the maintainer's copy; not in the repository. In copyright: only its methods and proportions
are used. This text and the pattern are our own.

**Threadloop (2026-11-15):** "salwar" finds no free patterns.

**Block:** none. Cut flat from straight lines (the `polygon` piece type, as the jumpsuit and aprons),
since this garment has no curved leg seams to take from a block. A hidden Titan part carries the
measurements. Drafted from the wearer's seat, waist to floor and ankle, so it fits any size.

**Status:** draft. Not sewn.

## What we took
The layout of a belted salwar, with a straight centre panel and tapering side panels (kalis) for each
leg, all gathered into a belt with a casing, at comparable proportions.

## How it maps onto the block
| Feature | Here (option default) |
|---|---|
| Length | `salwarLength` 96% of waist to floor, to the ankle |
| Bottom | `bottomWidth` 140% of the ankle, the centre panel's width |
| Belt | `beltDepth` 19.4%, `casingDepth` 5.6%, `beltWidth` 27.8% of the seat |
| Side panel | `kaliWidth` 50% of the seat, `crotchShare` 33.3% of the length below the belt, `kaliPoint` 2.1% of the seat |
| Patiala | The `patiala` variant: `kaliWidth` 62.5% |

## Changes and what isn't modelled
- **Seam allowances** are added by the pattern.
- **The side panels' ends** are straight lines.
- **The length** is a share of the wearer's waist to floor.
- **The hem** is plain; interface it for a stiffer finish. There's no ankle opening or cuff: the bottom
  is wide enough for the foot to pass.
- **Wider styles** beyond the Patiala are past `kaliWidth`'s range.
- **No tradition marker** yet: which to show is the maintainer's choice.

## Pieces
Centre panels (two, on the fold), side panels (four), belt (two, on the fold: front and back).

## Construction
1. Join a side panel to each long edge of each centre panel, straight edge to straight edge.
2. For the crotch seam, join the two legs' front panels along their top inner edges, then the back
   panels the same way.
3. On each leg, join the front side panel to the back one along the sloping edges for the inseam.
4. Join the belt pieces at the sides. Gather the legs' tops into the belt's lower edge.
5. Fold the casing over and thread a drawstring or elastic. Hem the bottoms.
