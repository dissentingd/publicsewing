# Three-piece long coat

**Source:** Merz, *Practical Cutting System for Ladies' Jackets and Cloaks* (1911), Diagram XVII, printed pp. 44–45, PDF pp. 46–47 (`merzspracticalcu00merz`). Public domain. **Status:** draft; not sewn.

## Source and draft

The text describes a semi-fitted front and back. It removes a quarter of the waist at each of two back positions, distributes hip additions around the side back, adds ¼ in at the back, and locates front suppression with ¾ in steps from points 10 and 11. The plate shows long shaped body divisions running through the waist to the hem, with a narrow back, side section and front. The diagonal construction and length-transfer lines are not additional seams. Neither page specifies finished length, button count, pockets, or sleeve proportions.

The draft extends the tailored jacket's continuous Breanna princess-panel construction. Its front, side front, side back and back panels approximate Merz's divisions; the existing panel geometry cannot place every point exactly. The plate's waist-to-hem length is about 1.15 times neck-to-waist, so `lengthBelowWaist` is 70% of waist-to-floor on the reference model. That proportion is read from the plate, not stated in the text. The 14% waist and 8% seat ease are our fit choices; the source's ⅜ in and ¼ in measurements are local drafting offsets, not finished ease. A fuller variant raises seat ease to 12%.

Merz does not draw a separate lapel, pocket or button placement. The draft closes the front to the neck by default, following the plate's high front edge. Its storm collar, front extension of about 1⅝ in on the reference size, three buttons, lining, facings and two-piece sleeve are practical additions. Pockets are omitted. A muslin is needed to assess fit, drape and the long seams before wearing.

## Construction

Join the shaped body panels from shoulder or armhole to hem. Sew shoulders, set the two-piece sleeves, then attach collar and front facings. Add the lining, hem the coat and finish the front closure. Check the long panel seam lengths and hem balance in a muslin before cutting final cloth.
