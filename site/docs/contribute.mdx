Most of our designs come from old drafting books. A well-made garment is a source too: its
pieces, proportions and construction can become a design that drafts to anyone's measurements.
If you own something worth keeping (a work jacket that has lasted, a good vintage coat, a shirt
your family made), you can copy it and send it to us.

You can also [sew a test](/sew-and-test) of a design that's already here, which helps just as much.

## Two ways to copy a garment

| | Rub-off | Deconstruction |
|---|---|---|
| **The garment** | Stays whole | Is taken apart |
| **Accuracy** | Good; takes care on curves and layered details | Best: you trace the real pieces |
| **Good for** | Anything you want to keep wearing | Worn-out garments and complicated tailoring |

### Rub-off

You copy each section of the garment while it's still sewn together.

1. Press the garment and do up its closures. Lay it on a pinnable surface or a table covered with
   muslin or paper.
2. Take one section at a time, such as half a back. Smooth it flat along the grain and pin along
   its seams.
3. Lay muslin over it and rub along the seams with wax tailor's chalk, or lay it over paper and
   push pins through the seams to mark the paper beneath.
4. Mark the grainline, darts, pleats, pocket positions, buttons and notches as you go.
5. Unpin, then true the lines with a ruler and curve. Sewn-together neighbours should still
   match: check that each seam is the same length as the seam it joins.
6. Note how much seam allowance you've added, if any.

Pleats and other layered details don't rub off well. Copy the base shape, then measure the
pleats and note them.

### Deconstruction

You open the seams and trace each piece.

1. Before you cut anything, photograph the garment from every side and inside out. Write down
   how it goes together, and in what order.
2. Mark the seamlines, the grainlines, and matching marks across seams, especially where a seam
   is eased, as at a sleeve cap. Label every piece.
3. If the garment is symmetrical, take apart only one side and keep the other for reference.
4. Open the seams gently with a seam ripper, main seams first, then collars, cuffs and pockets.
5. Press each piece lightly, without stretching it, and trace along the seamlines. Add nothing
   yet: send the seamlines.
6. Sew a muslin from your copy, if you can, and compare it with the original.

## Submit it {#submit}

**[Submit a garment on GitHub](https://github.com/dissentingd/publicsewing/issues/new?template=garment-submission.yml)**
(needs a free GitHub account). Everything in a GitHub issue is public.

The form asks for:

- **The pieces**, on any of: a PDF or DXF of your pattern, or photos taken straight down, with
  each piece flat beside a ruler. Say whether seam allowance is included.
- **Measurements of the finished garment**, flat: chest, waist, hem, length, sleeve and anything
  else that's characteristic, with your units. Numbers are what let us build a design from it.
- **Photos of the whole garment**, front, back and inside if you can.
- **How it's made**: the order of construction, its closures and pockets, and the fabric.
- **Where it came from**, as far as you know: its maker, its era, and whether it's one you made.

### What you need to agree to

So we can use your copy, you confirm that you made it yourself and license your pieces, notes
and photos under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). We'll credit you as
you ask. Please leave out logos, printed artwork and labels, as well as faces. If the garment is
a recent one from a current designer, say so, and we may decline it.

### What happens next

We read each submission and check it can carry a design: dimensioned pieces or real measurements,
not just photos. If it can, it's added to our [sources](/sources) with your credit, and a design
goes on the build queue. The design takes its pieces, proportions and construction from your
garment, and drafts them to the wearer's measurements on our blocks, so it won't be an exact copy
of the original. It starts as a *draft*, like every design here, until someone sews it.

We can't promise to build every submission, and if we can't use yours, we'll say why on the issue.
