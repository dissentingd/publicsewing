I'm not a programmer. I'm not a tailor.

But I can cook. Following instructions is easy.

Humans have been making clothes for 10,000s of years. We should be sharing. I can help.

But without these people, and their hard work, I would have nothing to play with, build upon, and
share with you.

Please give them all the due credit, as they are the ones that deserve it.

## The foundation {#foundation}

**[FreeSewing](https://freesewing.eu)**, created by **Joost De Cock** and built by
[its many contributors](https://codeberg.org/freesewing/freesewing/src/branch/develop/CONTRIBUTORS.md). Nearly every
pattern here is drawn on their blocks, the pattern tool on every design page is their editor, and the figures that show you how to measure
are theirs.
Years of careful, open, freely given work made it possible to draft a garment for anybody's body.
They gave it to the world under the MIT licence, and this site simply wouldn't exist without it.
PublicSewing isn't part of FreeSewing and isn't endorsed by it, but it owes them more than anyone.
If you enjoy this site, please go and thank them.

## The people who drew the garments {#garments}

In order of how many of our garments come from them. They taught cutters and sewers for a living,
and wrote it down so that others could learn. A century later, it still works.

**Harry Simons**, whose books give us more of this catalog than anyone else's. His coats, overcoats,
overalls and work clothes are clear, practical and generous with their numbers:
[*Drafting Cotton Garments and Mackinaws*](https://archive.org/details/draftingcottonga00simo) (1918),
[*Designing Overcoat Patterns for Men and Young Men*](https://archive.org/details/designingovercoa00simo) (1916),
[*Drafting Pants and Overalls*](https://archive.org/details/draftingpantsove00simo) (1916) and
[*Designing Boys' and Juveniles' Clothing*](https://archive.org/details/designingboysjuv00simo) (1922).

**[Open+Pattern](https://openpattern.io)**, the project of **Hugh Clarke** of TSOM | The Structure of
Magic, who find real garments, take them apart, digitise every piece and give the patterns away under
CC BY 4.0. What they do is extraordinary, and our [Inspired Designs](/inspired) are full of it. The
[full credit](/sources#open-pattern) lists each one. Please be kind and donate to them.

**The Jno. J. Mitchell Co.**, for
[*The New "Standard" Work on Designing and Pattern-Making of Ladies' Tailor-Made Garments*](https://archive.org/details/newstandardworko00mitc)
(1921), the source of our women's jackets, coats and skirts.

**Agnes Walker**, for the
[*Manual of Needlework and Cutting Out*](https://archive.org/details/krl00366374) (1909), written for
teachers of sewing. Our nightshirts, smocks, frocks and pinafores come from her.

**The authors of ЕМКО СЭВ**, vol. 3, *Base constructions of men's clothing* (Moscow, 1988), whose
methods and proportions inspired our suit, topcoat, winter coat and jackets. [More about how we
use it](/sources#emko).

**William D. F. Vincent**, for
[*The Cutter's Practical Guide to Cutting and Making Shirts, Undergarments, Collars, and Specialité
Clothing*](https://archive.org/details/vincentmakingshirts18xx) (1890): our shirts, pyjamas and
dressing gown.

**Esther Pivnick**, for
[*How to Design Beautiful Clothes*](https://archive.org/details/pivnickdesignclothes1949) (1949): our
shift dress, culottes, kimono dress and capes.

**William Merz**, for [*Merz's Practical Cutting System for Ladies' Jackets and
Cloaks*](https://archive.org/details/merzspracticalcu00merz) (1911): our women's peacoat and field
jacket.

**N. S. Sheifer**, for [*N. S. Sheifer's System of Designing and Grading Ladies', Misses' & Children's
Garments*](https://archive.org/details/nssheiferssystem00shei) (1908): our girl's coat.

**Mary Brooks Picken** and the **Woman's Institute of Domestic Arts and Sciences**, for
[*Pattern Drafting*](https://archive.org/details/patterdraftingpicken) (©1915): our shirtwaist dress.

**Thayaht** (Ernesto Michahelles), who designed the TuTa in Florence in 1920, the original of our
jumpsuit: a one-piece suit cut almost entirely from rectangles, and it still looks new.

## The people who measured {#measured}

**Ruth O'Brien** and her colleagues at the US Department of Agriculture, whose surveys
([children, 1939](https://archive.org/details/childrensbodymea365obri);
[boys and girls, 1941](https://archive.org/details/bodymeasurements366obri);
[women, 1941](https://archive.org/details/womensmeasuremen454obri)) give us our children's sizes, and
**everyone who stood still to be measured** for them.

## The people who kept the books {#archives}

**[The Internet Archive](https://archive.org)**, **[Google Books](https://books.google.com)** and
the **libraries and librarians** who kept these books for a hundred years, and scanned them so that
anyone could read them.

## The people who check our work {#checks}

**Maria Korosteleva** and **Olga Sorkine-Hornung**, for
[GarmentCode](https://github.com/maria-korosteleva/GarmentCode), and **Timur Levent Kesdogan, Fabian
Kemper, Stephan Wenninger, Jasmin Koller, Yuhan Zhang** and **Mario Botsch**, their co-authors on
GarmentCodeData. Their cloth simulator, with **NVIDIA's** [Warp](https://github.com/NVIDIA/warp),
makes the 3D drapes on our design pages.

**[Seamly2D](https://github.com/FashionFreedom/Seamly2D)** and its contributors, whose free pattern
software we test our exported files in.

**You, if you [sew a test](/sew-and-test).** Many of these haven't been sewn yet, so your name could go
here first.

## The tools {#tools}

The open-source projects this site is made from, and the people who maintain them for nothing:
[Docusaurus](https://github.com/facebook/docusaurus), [React](https://react.dev),
[Tailwind CSS](https://tailwindcss.com), [daisyUI](https://daisyui.com),
[patch-package](https://github.com/ds300/patch-package), [YAML](https://eemeli.org/yaml/),
[Playwright](https://playwright.dev) and [Node.js](https://nodejs.org).

**[Claude](https://claude.com)**, by Anthropic, wrote most of the code and the words, with some from
**Codex**, by OpenAI. The garment illustrations were made with an AI image model; [more about
those](/sources#illustrations). These tools learned from what a great many people wrote, and I'm
grateful to those people too.
