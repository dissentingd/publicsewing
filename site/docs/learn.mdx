New to sewing, or missing a skill? PublicSewing makes the pattern, and these lessons cover the
rest. We don't run any of these sites and aren't paid to list them.

## Where to learn

**[SewCraftly tutorials](https://sewcraftlypatterns.com/tutorials/)** are short, free, written
lessons:

- **Beginner basics:**
  [choosing fabric](https://sewcraftlypatterns.com/tutorials/how-to-choose-fabric-for-sewing-patterns/),
  [printing and assembling a PDF pattern](https://sewcraftlypatterns.com/tutorials/how-to-print-and-assemble-pdf-sewing-patterns/),
  [reading a pattern](https://sewcraftlypatterns.com/tutorials/how-to-read-a-sewing-pattern/),
  [taking body measurements](https://sewcraftlypatterns.com/tutorials/how-to-take-body-measurements/)
  and [choosing a size](https://sewcraftlypatterns.com/tutorials/how-to-choose-your-sewing-pattern-size/)
  (our patterns draft from your measurements, so you may not need this one)
- **Fabrics and fit:**
  [sewing stretch fabrics and knits](https://sewcraftlypatterns.com/tutorials/how-to-sew-stretch-fabrics/),
  [lengthening or shortening a pattern](https://sewcraftlypatterns.com/tutorials/how-to-lengthen-or-shorten-a-sewing-pattern/)
- **Techniques:**
  [fusible interfacing](https://sewcraftlypatterns.com/tutorials/how-to-use-fusible-interfacing/),
  [buttonholes and buttons](https://sewcraftlypatterns.com/tutorials/how-to-sew-buttonholes-and-buttons/),
  [zippers](https://sewcraftlypatterns.com/tutorials/how-to-sew-a-zipper/)

**[Professor Pincushion's Sewing Classroom](https://www.professorpincushion.com/sewing-classroom)**
has video lessons arranged from simple concepts to whole projects. The site lists over 500 free
videos, plus a paid tier.

**[Threads' Learn to Sew guide](https://www.threadsmagazine.com/project-guides/learn-to-sew)** goes
from choosing tools through construction, garment details and fitting, taught by experienced
instructors. Start with [Sewing Notions 101](https://www.threadsmagazine.com/project-guides/learn-to-sew/notions-101).
Some Threads articles need a paid membership.

**[FreeSewing's sewing terminology](https://freesewing.eu/docs/sewing/)** explains the terms used
in pattern instructions, one short page each.

**[*It's Fun to Sew with a Sewing Machine*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/61167)** is an old
4-H club booklet by Hildegarde Struefert, public domain in the US and free at Project Gutenberg.
It's dated, but the basics haven't changed.

## Index

The tools, materials and words you'll meet most often. Each entry can be linked to directly.

### Measuring and marking

<dl className="learn-index">
<Term name="Tape measure" img="/img/learn/tape-measure.jpg">A flexible tape for measuring bodies and curved seams. Fibreglass tapes don't stretch.</Term>
<Term name="Clear ruler" img="/img/learn/clear-ruler.jpg">A see-through ruler for drawing straight lines and adding seam allowance to a pattern.</Term>
<Term name="French curve" img="/img/learn/french-curve.jpg">A curved template for drawing and correcting necklines, armholes and other curves.</Term>
<Term name="Tailor's chalk" img="/img/learn/tailors-chalk.jpg">Chalk or wax for marking fabric. Wax can leave a mark that needs pressing or washing out, so test it on a scrap.</Term>
<Term name="Marking pen" img="/img/learn/marking-pen.jpg">A fabric pen whose ink washes or fades away. Test it first: heat can set some inks.</Term>
<Term name="Tracing wheel" img="/img/learn/tracing-wheel.jpg">A toothed wheel that transfers lines through a pattern, or through carbon paper, onto the fabric below.</Term>
</dl>

### Cutting

<dl className="learn-index">
<Term name="Shears" img="/img/learn/shears.jpg">Long-bladed scissors with a bent handle, so the fabric stays flat as you cut. Keep them for fabric only: paper dulls them.</Term>
<Term name="Paper scissors" img="/img/learn/paper-scissors.jpg">An ordinary pair for cutting out patterns.</Term>
<Term name="Thread snips" img="/img/learn/thread-snips.jpg">Small spring-loaded clippers for trimming thread ends.</Term>
<Term name="Rotary cutter" img="/img/learn/rotary-cutter.jpg">A round blade, used on a self-healing mat. It's quick on long straight edges.</Term>
<Term name="Pattern weights" img="/img/learn/pattern-weights.jpg">Anything heavy and flat that holds a pattern on the fabric in place of pins.</Term>
</dl>

### Sewing

<dl className="learn-index">
<Term name="Sewing machine" img="/img/learn/sewing-machine.jpg">A straight stitch and a zigzag will sew every garment here. A one-step buttonhole is a welcome extra.</Term>
<Term name="Machine needle" img="/img/learn/machine-needle.jpg">Sized by number, with larger numbers for heavier fabric. Use a universal or sharp needle for wovens, and a ballpoint or stretch needle for knits. Replace it when it gets dull.</Term>
<Term name="Hand needle" img="/img/learn/hand-needle.jpg">Sharps are for general sewing, betweens are short ones for fine stitches, and darners are long ones for basting.</Term>
<Term name="Pins" img="/img/learn/pins.jpg">Hold layers together until they're sewn. Glass-headed pins survive the iron.</Term>
<Term name="Thread" img="/img/learn/thread.jpg">All-purpose polyester suits most projects. Use a heavier thread for topstitching and jeans.</Term>
<Term name="Serger" img="/img/learn/serger.jpg">Also called an overlocker: a machine that sews a seam, trims it and wraps the edge in one pass. It's useful but not essential.</Term>
<Term name="Seam ripper" img="/img/learn/seam-ripper.jpg">A small forked blade for unpicking stitches. You'll use it, and so does everyone else.</Term>
<Term name="Thimble" img="/img/learn/thimble.jpg">Protects the finger that pushes a hand needle through heavy cloth.</Term>
</dl>

### Pressing

<dl className="learn-index">
<Term name="Iron" img="/img/learn/iron.jpg">Press each seam before crossing it with another. Steam helps wool and cotton take shape.</Term>
<Term name="Press cloth" img="/img/learn/press-cloth.jpg">A piece of cotton or silk organza between iron and fabric, which prevents shine and scorching.</Term>
<Term name="Tailor's ham" img="/img/learn/tailors-ham.jpg">A firm, rounded cushion for pressing curves, such as darts and sleeve caps, without flattening them.</Term>
<Term name="Sleeve board" img="/img/learn/sleeve-board.jpg">A small ironing board that fits inside a sleeve.</Term>
<Term name="Clapper" img="/img/learn/clapper.jpg">A block of hardwood, held on a steamed seam until it cools, so the seam sets flat. Tailors use it on wool.</Term>
</dl>

### Fabric and notions

<dl className="learn-index">
<Term name="Woven" img="/img/learn/woven.jpg">Fabric made of crossed threads. It has little stretch and frays when cut. Unless a design says otherwise, patterns here are for wovens.</Term>
<Term name="Knit" img="/img/learn/knit.jpg">Fabric made of interlocked loops. It stretches and rarely frays, and is what T-shirts are made from.</Term>
<Term name="Muslin" img="/img/learn/muslin.jpg">Cheap plain cotton for test garments (in the UK, calico). The test garment itself is also called a muslin, or a toile. See [Sew & Test](/sew-and-test).</Term>
<Term name="Interfacing" img="/img/learn/interfacing.jpg">An extra layer that stiffens collars, cuffs and front edges, either sewn in or fused on with heat.</Term>
<Term name="Lining" img="/img/learn/lining.jpg">A separate inner layer that hides the seams and helps a coat slide on.</Term>
<Term name="Twill tape" img="/img/learn/twill-tape.jpg">A narrow, strong woven tape, used to keep seams and edges from stretching.</Term>
<Term name="Bias tape" img="/img/learn/bias-tape.jpg">A strip cut on the diagonal so it curves smoothly, used to bind edges.</Term>
</dl>

### On a pattern

<dl className="learn-index">
<Term name="Grainline" img="/img/learn/grainline.jpg">An arrow on a pattern piece. Lay it parallel to the fabric's selvedge so the garment hangs straight.</Term>
<Term name="Selvedge" img="/img/learn/selvedge.jpg">The firm, finished edge along each side of a length of fabric.</Term>
<Term name="Bias" img="/img/learn/bias.jpg">The diagonal across the grain, where a woven stretches and drapes most.</Term>
<Term name="Notch" img="/img/learn/notch.jpg">A small mark on a seam edge, matched with its partner when you sew.</Term>
<Term name="Cut on fold" img="/img/learn/cut-on-fold.jpg">The piece's edge goes on a fold in the fabric, which makes one symmetrical piece from half a pattern.</Term>
<Term name="Seam allowance" img="/img/learn/seam-allowance.jpg">The fabric between the stitching and the cut edge. Our pattern tool can add it for you; check whether it's on before you cut.</Term>
<Term name="Ease" img="/img/learn/ease.jpg">The difference between the garment and the body, for movement and style. Also, the slight extra length that lets a sleeve cap fit its armhole.</Term>
<Term name="Dart" img="/img/learn/dart.jpg">A stitched wedge that turns flat cloth around a curve of the body.</Term>
<Term name="Block" img="/img/learn/block.jpg">A basic fitted pattern that other designs are built from. Ours are FreeSewing's, drafted from your measurements.</Term>
<Term name="Drafting" img="/img/learn/drafting.jpg">Drawing a pattern from measurements, rather than scaling up a fixed size.</Term>
</dl>

### Garment parts

<dl className="learn-index">
<Term name="Yoke" img="/img/learn/yoke.jpg">A fitted piece across the shoulders or hips, with the body of the garment sewn to it.</Term>
<Term name="Facing" img="/img/learn/facing.jpg">A piece turned to the inside to finish an edge such as a neckline or coat front.</Term>
<Term name="Placket" img="/img/learn/placket.jpg">A finished opening with an overlap, as at a shirt front or cuff.</Term>
<Term name="Sleeve cap" img="/img/learn/sleeve-cap.jpg">The rounded top of a sleeve, sewn into the armhole.</Term>
<Term name="Lapel" img="/img/learn/lapel.jpg">The part of a coat front that folds back below the collar.</Term>
<Term name="Welt" img="/img/learn/welt.jpg">A narrow strip finishing a set-in pocket's opening.</Term>
<Term name="Vent" img="/img/learn/vent.jpg">An open slit at a hem, usually at the back of a coat, that gives room to move.</Term>
<Term name="Rise" img="/img/learn/rise.jpg">On trousers, the distance from the crotch to the waist.</Term>
</dl>

### Sewing terms

<dl className="learn-index">
<Term name="Right sides together" img="/img/learn/right-sides-together.jpg">The usual way to sew a seam: the outside faces of both pieces face each other.</Term>
<Term name="Baste" img="/img/learn/baste.jpg">Sew long temporary stitches, by hand or machine, to test or hold something.</Term>
<Term name="Staystitch" img="/img/learn/staystitch.jpg">A line of stitching just inside a curved edge, sewn early so the edge doesn't stretch.</Term>
<Term name="Clip" img="/img/learn/clip.jpg">Snip into the seam allowance of an inward curve so it lies flat when turned. On outward curves, cut small wedges out instead.</Term>
<Term name="Understitch" img="/img/learn/understitch.jpg">Stitch the facing to the seam allowance beside the seam, so the facing stays out of sight.</Term>
<Term name="Topstitch" img="/img/learn/topstitch.jpg">Stitching that shows on the outside, for strength or looks. Close to the edge, it's called edgestitching.</Term>
<Term name="Gather" img="/img/learn/gather.jpg">Pull up long stitches to fit a longer edge into a shorter one.</Term>
<Term name="Seam finish" img="/img/learn/seam-finish.jpg">Anything that stops a cut edge fraying: zigzag, overlocking, pinking or binding.</Term>
<Term name="French seam" img="/img/learn/french-seam.jpg">A seam sewn twice, wrong sides together and then right sides together, which encloses the raw edges. It's neat on light fabrics.</Term>
<Term name="Flat-felled seam" img="/img/learn/flat-felled-seam.jpg">One seam allowance wrapped around the other and stitched down. It's the strong seam on jeans and work shirts.</Term>
<Term name="Hem" img="/img/learn/hem.jpg">The folded and stitched lower edge of a garment or sleeve.</Term>
</dl>
