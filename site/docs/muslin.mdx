title: Print and sew a muslin
sidebar_position: 4
---

A muslin (a toile) is a test garment in cheap fabric. For an untested draft it's the only way to
know whether the pattern fits.

## Print

1. On the design's page, choose your measurements, then open **Export Pattern** and pick
   **A4 PDF** or **Letter PDF**.
2. Print **at 100% / actual size**. Turn off "fit to page" and any scaling.
3. Find the **test square**: a box on its own small piece, marked PublicSewing. Measure it: it
   should match the size printed beside it, in both directions. If it doesn't, fix the print
   settings before going on.
4. Trim or fold the margins and tape the tiles together, matching the marks at the edges of
   each page. The cover page lists the settings and measurements used; keep it for your report.

## Cut

- Use calico or an old sheet of about the weight of the real fabric.
- Seam allowance is included only if it was on in the pattern tool (*Core settings*); check
  before cutting. Transfer notches, grainlines, centre lines and the waist and chest lines.
- Lining, facings and pockets can be left out of a fit muslin. Collars and sleeves can't: they
  test the neck and the armhole.

## Sew and check

Baste with long stitches so you can let seams out. Try it on over what you'd wear under the
garment, then check:

- **Balance:** do the hems hang level, and the side seams straight down? Does the front stay
  closed without pulling or swinging open?
- **Ease:** can you reach forward, sit, lift your arms? Too tight or too loose at chest, waist,
  seat?
- **Neck and shoulders:** does the collar sit on the neck? Shoulder seams on top of the
  shoulder, ending at the shoulder point?
- **Armhole and sleeve:** does the cap go in without puckers (allowing for the ease the design
  states)? Can you move? Does the sleeve hang without twisting?
- **Seams that should match:** do they? If one is longer than its partner, note which and by
  how much.
- **Length:** body, sleeve, and (for trousers) the rise.

Mark changes on the muslin with a pen, pin out excess, and photograph front, side and back.

## Report it

[Report what happened](/report), good or bad. A report with photos and the settings from the
cover page is the most useful thing you can give the project.
