## 1. Get ready {#prepare}

Choose a [design](/designs) and read its **Pieces** and **Construction** sections. You can use a
size set or [take your own measurements](/measure). You will need:

- Inexpensive <a href="/learn#woven">woven fabric</a>, such as <a href="/learn#muslin">calico (muslin)</a> or an old sheet, close in weight to the fabric you plan to use.
- A printer and paper, tape, <a href="/learn#paper-scissors">paper scissors</a>, <a href="/learn#pins">pins</a>, <a href="/learn#thread">thread</a> and a <a href="/learn#sewing-machine">sewing machine</a> (or a <a href="/learn#hand-needle">hand needle</a>).
- A <a href="/learn#tape-measure">tape measure</a> and a <a href="/learn#marking-pen">marking pen</a> or <a href="/learn#tailor-s-chalk">tailor's chalk</a> for your practice fabric.

:::tip[New to sewing?]
The [Learn page](/learn) has short lessons on fabric, printing patterns, reading markings and
basic stitching. It is fine to learn one step at a time.
:::

## 2. Print the pattern {#print}

On the design page, pick a size or enter your measurements, then open **Export Pattern**. Choose
the printing route that works for you:

<div className="test-checks">
  <div><h3>Print at home</h3><p>Choose <strong>A4 PDF</strong> or <strong>Letter PDF</strong> to match your paper. Print at <strong>100% / Actual size</strong>, with <strong>Fit to page</strong> turned off. Print the test-square page first. When it measures correctly, print the rest and tape the tiles together at their matching marks. Keep the PDF cover page with your size and settings.</p></div>
  <div><h3>Use a print shop</h3><p>Choose a large-format PDF that your local shop accepts. For FedEx Office, use the dedicated <strong>FedEx Office PDF (36 × 48 in)</strong> button, then upload that file to <a href="https://www.office.fedex.com/default/architecture-construction-engineering.html">FedEx Office blueprint printing</a>. Ask for black and white, 36 × 48 in on standard white bond paper, no binding, vertical orientation, and <strong>actual size (100%)</strong> with fit-to-page off. Options may vary by location.</p></div>
</div>

**Check the print before cutting.** Find the test square on the pattern and measure both sides with
a ruler. They should match the measurement printed beside it. If a home print is wrong, fix the
scaling and reprint that page. If a shop print is wrong, ask the shop to reprint at actual size.
The FedEx file contains pattern sheets and the scale-check square, but no cover page: write down
your chosen size and settings for your report.

<div className="test-callout"><strong>Why check the square?</strong> A small printing error changes every pattern piece. Checking one page can save a whole practice garment.</div>

## 3. Cut and sew a practice version {#cut-and-sew}

Check **Core settings** in the pattern tool before cutting: <a href="/learn#seam-allowance">seam allowance</a>
is included only when that setting is on. It is the extra fabric outside the line where you sew.

Transfer the pattern's <a href="/learn#notch">notches</a> (small matching marks), <a href="/learn#grainline">grainlines</a>, centre lines and waist or chest
lines onto the fabric. Cut the pieces listed on the design page. For a first fit check, you can
usually leave out pockets, <a href="/learn#lining">lining</a> and some <a href="/learn#facing">facings</a>. Keep the collar and sleeves if the garment has
them: they affect how the neck and armholes fit.

Follow the design page's **Construction** order. Sew with long, easy-to-remove stitches (called
<a href="/learn#baste">basting</a>). This lets you adjust a seam and try again.

## 4. Check the fit {#check-fit}

Try the garment on over the clothes you would normally wear underneath. Look in a mirror, or ask
someone to help. Check the front, side and back. You do not have to diagnose every wrinkle; write
down what you can see and feel.

<div className="test-checks">
  <div><h3>Does it hang straight?</h3><p>Are the hems level and the side seams roughly vertical? Does the front stay closed without pulling or swinging open?</p></div>
  <div><h3>Can you move?</h3><p>Reach forward, sit and lift your arms. <a href="/learn#ease">Ease</a> is the room added beyond your body measurements. Where does it feel too tight or too loose?</p></div>
  <div><h3>Do the neck and sleeves sit well?</h3><p>Does the collar rest on the neck? Do shoulder seams sit on top of the shoulders? Do sleeves hang without twisting, and can you move your arms?</p></div>
  <div><h3>Are the lengths right?</h3><p>Check the body and sleeve lengths, and the <a href="/learn#rise">rise</a> on trousers. If two seams meant to join are different lengths, note which ones and by how much.</p></div>
</div>

Pin out extra fabric or mark where more room is needed. A pen mark on a practice garment is fine.
Front, side and back photos can help you compare changes, but you do not need to share them.

## 5. Tell us what happened {#report}

Reports help us find problems that code checks cannot see. A good result helps too. The report
form asks which design and size you used, what fabric you chose, what fit, and what did not. You can
write “nothing noticed” where that is true.

<div className="test-report-card">
  <h3>Send a muslin report</h3>
  <p>The form opens on GitHub, a public project site. You need a free GitHub account to submit it. The report is public, so you can leave out body measurements and photos, or crop faces from photos.</p>
  <a className="button button--primary" href="https://github.com/dissentingd/publicsewing/issues/new?template=muslin-report.yml">Open the report form on GitHub</a>
</div>

**Example:** “I made the work shirt using the Men 40 size set and an old cotton sheet. The chest
felt comfortable, but the sleeves pulled when I reached forward. I did not change the design
options. I added 2 cm at the upper arm for a second try.”

If you have not used GitHub before, the form is a page with boxes to fill in. Sign in or create an
account, choose the design from its list, fill in the required boxes, then select **Submit new
issue**. Photos and exact measurements are optional. We record reported results in the design's
brief; a design is marked *tested* once a sewn muslin has been reported.
