## Sources {#sources}

Most designs draw on five drafting books, all in the public domain in the United States:

- Simons, *Drafting Cotton Garments and Mackinaws* (New York, 1918);
- Simons, *Designing Overcoat Patterns for Men and Young Men* (New York, 1916);
- Walker, *Manual of Needlework and Cutting Out* (London, 1909);
- Woman's Institute, *Pattern Drafting* (Scranton, ©1915);
- Mitchell, *The New "Standard" Work on Designing and Pattern-Making of Ladies' Tailor-Made
  Garments* (New York, 1921).

The windbreaker, quilted jacket, winter coat, topcoat, suit jacket, waistcoat and suit trousers are
inspired by [ЕМКО СЭВ](#emko).

The jumpsuit, T-shirt, anorak and field trousers are inspired by patterns from
[Open+Pattern](#open-pattern).

Children's sizes come from the USDA's O'Brien surveys (1939), a US government work.

Drafting methods, formulas, proportions and body measurements aren't copyrightable; the
expression of a book (its text, layout and illustrations) is. Each design takes its pieces,
proportions and construction from a book, gets its geometry from FreeSewing's blocks, and is
written in our own words. Scans of the public-domain books, with each one's origin and rights
basis, are in the [repository](https://github.com/dissentingd/publicsewing/tree/main/corpus).

## Open+Pattern {#open-pattern}

[Open+Pattern](https://openpattern.io) digitises real garments and publishes the patterns under
[CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Everything of theirs stays under that
licence. Open+Pattern didn't make or endorse PublicSewing or anything on this site.

**Our interpretations.** These designs are inspired by their patterns. Each is our own
interpretation: we took its pieces, proportions and construction, and our tool redrafts them from
the wearer's measurements, so it differs from the original. Each design's brief lists what changed.

| Our design | Inspired by |

| [Field trousers](/designs/field-trousers) | [British Army CS95 Field Combat Trouser](https://openpattern.io/product/british-army-cs95-field-combat-trouser/) |
| [Jumpsuit](/designs/jumpsuit) | [TuTa Jumpsuit](https://openpattern.io/product/tuta-jumpsuit-thayaht-1920/), after Thayaht (1920) |

**Their own patterns.** Entries tagged *External* on [Inspired Designs](/inspired) are Open+Pattern's
patterns, linked and not hosted here. They come in fixed sizes, not drafted to your measurements.
You download them from their site, at a price you choose (€0 is allowed), on a computer rather than
a phone; check their page for what the checkout asks for.

## ЕМКО СЭВ {#emko}

Seven designs on [Inspired Designs](/inspired) are inspired by ЕМКО СЭВ, vol. 3, *Base constructions
of men's clothing* (Moscow, 1988): the [windbreaker](/designs/windbreaker), [quilted
jacket](/designs/quilted-jacket), [winter coat](/designs/winter-coat), [topcoat](/designs/topcoat),
[suit jacket](/designs/suit-jacket), [waistcoat](/designs/waistcoat) and [suit
trousers](/designs/suit-trousers).

The book is still in copyright, so it isn't in our repository. Each design is our own interpretation:
it takes only the book's methods, proportions and construction, and not its text or figures. Its
geometry is redrafted from the wearer's measurements. The book's authors and publisher didn't make
or endorse these designs.

## Built on FreeSewing {#freesewing}

The pattern geometry comes from [FreeSewing](https://freesewing.org)'s blocks (Brian, Bent,
Breanna, Titan), and the pattern tool on each design page is FreeSewing's editor, with small
changes. Both are MIT-licensed, © Joost De Cock and the FreeSewing contributors. PublicSewing is
not part of FreeSewing and isn't endorsed by it.

## Illustrations {#illustrations}

The pictures of finished garments at the top of the design pages are generated with an AI
image model from a description of each design at its default options. They show what the
garment looks like, but they aren't drawn from the pattern and may differ from it in detail;
the pattern and its brief are the reference. Machine-generated images generally can't be
copyrighted, so the licence below doesn't cover them.

## Privacy {#privacy}

No accounts, no cookies, no analytics, no server. Measurements you enter are kept in your
browser's local storage and never sent anywhere.
