# Learn index image specs

One small image per term on the [Learn](../docs/learn.mdx) page, saved as
`site/static/img/learn/<term>.jpg` (square, 768 px). Each entry says what the picture must show
for the term to read at thumbnail size; the **bold first line** is the test an image has to pass.
Written 2026-11-09 from each term's definition on the Learn page. When a definition changes,
update its entry here.

These cover the pattern, garment-part and sewing terms. The 31 tools and materials were made
before this file and have no entry yet.

## Shared staging

> One sewing sample, pattern piece or garment detail, alone on a plain bare wooden table, centred,
> seen from slightly above, the whole of it in frame with margin all round. Painted as a
> mid-century American Realist oil painting: visible, solid brushstrokes, strong low raking
> sunlight from one side, hard-edged shadows, muted colours with warm light and cool shadows.
> No text, labels, logo, people or hands. Square format.

**Every image:** the sample is cut or sewn the way a tailor would, at a size where the one feature
that names the term is the largest, clearest thing in the picture. Plain cloth (cream, blue or
dark wool) unless the term needs a print; stitching in a contrasting thread when the stitching is
the point.

## On a pattern

### grainline: Grainline
> **A long double-headed arrow on a paper pattern piece, running parallel to the selvedge of the
> cloth beneath.** A brown paper pattern piece lies on a length of plain woven cloth; its
> selvedge edge is visible along one side, and the arrow is drawn in the middle of the paper,
> clearly parallel to it.
>
> Leave out: a single-headed arrow, a ruler standing in for the arrow, printed words on the paper.

### selvedge: Selvedge
> **One swatch of woven cloth with a firm, finished factory edge along one side, contrasting
> with a cut, fraying edge.** The selvedge is a narrow, dense, straight border running the
> swatch's full length; the opposite edge is cut and shows loose threads.
>
> Leave out: two pieces of cloth, a hemmed or stitched edge passed off as the selvedge.

### bias: Bias
> **A strip cut diagonally across a square of woven cloth, bending in a smooth curve beside it.**
> The square shows its straight threads (a visible check or weave helps), and the strip, cut at
> 45 degrees to them, lies curved to show how the bias stretches and drapes.
>
> Leave out: a folded square, a straight strip cut along the threads, bias tape on a reel.

### notch: Notch
> **Two pattern or cloth pieces whose seam edges each carry one small notch, lined up where they
> will be sewn.** The notches are small triangles cut into the edges, matched across the gap.
>
> Leave out: many notches, holes punched in the middle of the pieces.

### cut-on-fold: Cut on fold
> **A half-pattern laid with its straight edge exactly on the fold of doubled cloth.** The fold
> runs down one side, clearly a fold rather than a cut edge, and the paper's straight edge sits
> right on it from top to bottom.
>
> Leave out: a pattern piece floating away from the fold, two separate cut layers.

### seam-allowance: Seam allowance
> **Two pieces of cloth sewn together and opened flat, with a clear strip of cloth between the
> stitch line and the cut edges.** The stitching runs parallel to the raw edges, in a
> contrasting thread.
>
> Leave out: a pressed-open seam seen only from the right side, where the allowance is hidden.

### ease: Ease
> **A longer edge eased smoothly into a shorter one, with soft fullness and no pleats.** Two
> strips of cloth sewn along one edge: the upper one is visibly longer and its extra length is
> taken up as gentle ripples along the seam.
>
> Leave out: sharp pleats or tucks, gathers bunched tight, two strips of the same length.

### dart: Dart
> **A narrow stitched wedge that tapers to a point, shaping flat cloth into a slight curve.** A
> swatch of plain cloth with one dart sewn into it, rising from one edge to a fine point, the
> cloth around the point lifting a little.
>
> Leave out: a pleat or open slit, a dart sewn on paper, several darts.

### block: Block
> **Plain basic bodice pattern pieces, a front and a back, with no style lines.** Two flat paper
> pieces with shoulder, armhole, side seam and waist, each with a simple waist dart.
>
> Leave out: collars, buttons, pockets or style seams drawn on, a dress form.

### drafting: Drafting
> **A pattern being drawn on paper: straight construction lines and one finished curved
> outline, with a ruler and a French curve beside it.** The draft is part done, so both the
> pencil grid and the outline show.
>
> Leave out: a printed pattern, a cutting mat, scissors cutting.

## On a garment

### yoke: Yoke
> **A separate shaped panel across the shoulders of a shirt back, with the body sewn to it along
> one seam.** The shirt lies flat, back up; the yoke seam runs straight across below the
> shoulders.
>
> Leave out: a front view, pockets, a box pleat stealing attention from the seam.

### facing: Facing
> **A neckline turned to show the inside, where a shaped strip of cloth finishes the edge.** A
> contrasting colour for the facing reads best at thumbnail size. The facing follows the curve of the neckline, a few fingers wide, its outer edge
> finished and lying flat on the inside of the garment.
>
> Leave out: binding wrapped over the edge, a ribbed knit neckband, a T-shirt.

### placket: Placket
> **A finished opening with one edge overlapping the other, buttoned.** A shirt front or sleeve
> placket: two folded strips, one over the other, with buttons and buttonholes down it.
>
> Leave out: a zip, a single raw slit.

### sleeve-cap: Sleeve cap
> **A sleeve pattern piece whose top is a tall, rounded, symmetrical bell curve.** The paper
> piece lies flat, cap at the top; its two underarm seams run down to a narrower straight wrist
> edge, the whole piece symmetrical about its centre line.
>
> Leave out: a lopsided or one-sided curve, a whole garment, a sleeve set into an armhole.

### lapel: Lapel
> **A coat front folded back below the collar, the notch between collar and lapel clear.** The
> upper front of a plain wool coat, lapel turned back to show its underside.
>
> Leave out: a shawl collar, buttons on the lapel, a tie or shirt.

### welt: Welt
> **A narrow, raised strip of cloth finishing the opening of a set-in pocket.** A close view of a
> wool coat front with one straight welt pocket.
>
> Leave out: a pocket flap, a patch pocket, a zip.

### vent: Vent
> **An open slit at the back hem of a coat, its two finished edges overlapping and parted a
> little.** The lower back of a plain coat, the vent rising from the hem.
>
> Leave out: two side vents, a pleat, a belt.

### rise: Rise
> **Trousers seen flat from the side or front with the span from crotch seam up to the waistband
> made obvious.** A tape measure laid from the crotch point straight up to the waistband shows
> the distance.
>
> Leave out: turn-ups, a tape measured down the leg, a person.

## Sewing terms

### right-sides-together: Right sides together
> **Two pieces of printed cloth stacked face to face, one corner peeled back to show the printed
> faces meeting inside.** The plain backs face out; pins hold the edges.
>
> Leave out: pieces side by side, prints facing out.

### baste: Baste
> **Long, widely spaced temporary stitches in a bright contrasting thread holding two layers
> together.** A needle and a loose thread end lie beside the row.
>
> Leave out: short machine stitches, pins in place of stitches.

### staystitch: Staystitch
> **A single layer of cloth with a curved cut edge, and one line of small stitches just inside
> the curve.** The edge is raw and unfolded; the stitching follows it a finger-width in.
>
> Leave out: a folded or hemmed edge, a second layer, a facing.

### clip: Clip
> **A stitched inward curve whose seam allowance has several small snips cut toward the
> stitching, stopping just short of it.** The snips are clearly cuts in the cloth, evenly
> spaced along the curve.
>
> Leave out: notches cut out as wedges, snips crossing the stitching, scissors.

### understitch: Understitch
> **A facing folded open beside its seam, with a second row of stitches on the facing close to
> the seam, catching the seam allowance underneath.** Seen from the inside, the understitching
> in a contrasting thread.
>
> Leave out: topstitching seen from the outside, a single row of stitches.

### topstitch: Topstitch
> **One crisp, even row of contrasting stitches on the outside of the cloth, parallel to a
> finished edge.**
>
> Leave out: stitching on the inside, zigzag stitch.

### gather: Gather
> **A strip of cloth drawn up into many small even folds by rows of long stitches, sewn to a
> shorter straight band.**
>
> Leave out: crisp knife pleats, elastic, smocking.

### seam-finish: Seam finish
> **Three cut seam edges side by side, each kept from fraying a different way: zigzag,
> overlocking and pinking or binding.**
>
> Leave out: one sample only, a raw fraying edge with no finish.

### french-seam: French seam
> **A narrow seam in sheer cloth, turned to show the inside: a slim folded tube enclosing the raw
> edges, held by a row of stitches.**
>
> Leave out: visible raw edges, overlocking, heavy cloth.

### flat-felled-seam: Flat-felled seam
> **A flat seam in sturdy cloth with no raw edges showing and two parallel rows of stitching down
> it, as on jeans.**
>
> Leave out: a single row of stitching, a raised ridge, a whole pair of jeans.

### hem: Hem
> **The lower edge of a garment turned up to the inside and stitched, with the fold and the
> stitch line both visible.**
>
> Leave out: a raw edge, binding, a whole garment.
